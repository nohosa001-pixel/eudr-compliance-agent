Frequently Asked Questions

Essential information on Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 compliance, satellite audits, and TRACES-NT filing.

What is EUDR (Regulation (EU) 2023/1115) and who is affected?
The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR, Regulation EU 2023/1115) requires companies placing cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya, and wood into the EU market to prove their supply chains are deforestation-free and legally harvested. It applies to both large multinational operators and SME traders handling covered raw materials or derived products.
What is the Dec 31, 2020 deforestation cutoff date?
Under EUDR Article 2, commodities must originate from land that has not been subject to deforestation or forest degradation after December 31, 2020. EUDRAgent.com runs automated Sentinel-2, Sentinel-1 radar, and Hansen Global Forest Change satellite scans to rigorously verify canopy cover before and after this cutoff date.
What are the 4-hectare geolocation and polygon requirements?
For any production plot exceeding 4 hectares (ha), operators must provide accurate polygon boundary coordinates (latitude/longitude with at least 6 decimal places). Plots 4ha or smaller may use a single point coordinate. EUDR.agent provides autonomous geometric topology healing that fixes self-intersections and vertex winding issues in under 2 seconds.
How does TRACES-NT XML Due Diligence Statement (DDS) generation work?
EUDR.agent automatically parses your supply chain data (CSV, GeoJSON, Shapefile, or API), validates legal harvest licenses and satellite radar integrity, and generates cryptographic, schema-valid TRACES-NT XML files ready for 1-click submission to the European Commission customs portal.
Can suppliers directly upload their farm coordinates?
Yes. With our dedicated Supplier Onboarding Portal, suppliers and cooperative managers can upload farm plots in bulk and receive instant pre-clearance validation without needing complex GIS software.
What penalties do companies face for EUDR non-compliance?
Penalties under Article 25 include mandatory confiscation of goods, temporary exclusion from EU public procurement, and maximum fines of at least 4% of the operator's total annual EU turnover, in addition to severe customs port impoundments.
